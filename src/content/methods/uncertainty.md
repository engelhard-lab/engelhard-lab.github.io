Clinical decision support systems must not only predict risk, but also communicate how confident they are in a given prediction. We focus on second-order uncertainty, which is our uncertainty about the distribution we ought to predict, including methods to evaluate the discrimination performance of uncertainty-aware risk prediction models and to quantify uncertainty in longitudinal settings with complex modalities such as images and text. Accurate uncertainty quantification helps us reason about how predictions might change over time, promote trust, and ensure uncertain predictions do not influence clinical decisions.
